Train the people, run the work, and keep the evidence.

OnTrak is one repository for the IT support operation: a browser-based training platform that grades work against deliberately broken machines, a service desk that runs the real tickets and carries an insurer-grade evidence trail through incident response — and, after both, the identity and intrusion protection they share. Every part is self-hosted, every privileged act is audited, and the record that survives is one an auditor can verify.

Why OnTrak

The answers that decide whether the platform is usable on a real desk.

ProblemOnTrak answer
Practice ranges are built for a demo, not for a class — and every rebuild is another day of work. Simulations are authored once as versioned templates with checkable objectives; a student gets a fresh clone per attempt and the same grader every time.
"Did they fix it?" graded by hand does not scale, and two instructors grade differently. Objectives are evaluated against live system state on the server. Any correct fix passes — including the ones the author did not think of — and the score is recomputed server-side, never trusted from the browser.
A single-desk helpdesk breaks the moment it serves a second client: work runs together and SLAs stop meaning anything. Clients, contacts, per-client SLAs and per-client rate cards, with a scope that shows an agent only the clients they are assigned to — and work that names no client stays visible to the whole desk.
An incident that becomes a claim, an audit or a lawsuit needs a record that was not assembled after the fact. Incident evidence is written under object lock, notification wording is recorded as it was actually sent, and the assurance packet is signed — so the story cannot be edited later, by anyone.
Billing rebuilt from a timesheet is a dispute waiting to happen. Hours are priced by a snapshot stored on the entry itself, invoices are stamped once and cannot be issued twice, and a credit note is the only path back.
Training, ticketing and security tooling each invent their own identity and evidence layer. One identity layer, one evidence format, one audit chain: the training record and the incident packet an insurer reads are built from the same signed, tamper-evident shape.

Inside OnTrak Tix

The desk is the part most teams feel first, and the part with the most to get wrong.

A worklist that respects a client boundary

Inbox, saved views and SLA flags are all computed after the client scope is applied, so an agent cannot be shown a figure that includes a client they cannot open. Acting as a client is a permission, a reason on the record and one expiring window.

Promises written down, not remembered

An SLA policy belongs to a client, a queue or the desk, and one resolver answers most-specific-first — reporting which rung won. Writing a promise is validated before it is in force and refused while tickets are still measured against it.

Incident response with evidence that holds

Artifacts land under object lock, retention has a clock the desk cannot wind back early, notification wording is kept as sent, and the packet is signed. A scheduled sweep purges what the window has closed and records what it purged.

Hours that add up to an invoice

Time is logged where the work is, priced by the client's rate card or the desk default with the rate snapshotted onto the entry, and exported as invoice-ready lines. Issuing an invoice freezes the entries it covers; a credit note is the remedy.

Reporting that cannot disagree with itself

The desk-wide report and every client's scorecard come from one function, with a row for work that names no client — so the parts add up to the whole, and the first row is the one somebody can act on.

Every privileged act on the chain

Sign-in, role changes, exports, purges, invoices, surveys and act-as windows are append-only, hash-chained audit events. If it mattered, it is in the record — and the record can be verified.

Inside OnTrak IT Support Training

Practice that is graded by the machine, not by a mark scheme.

Simulations behind one interface

Linux shell, Windows desktop and Office applications each implement the same driver contract, so a scenario author writes objectives once and the grader runs them the same way everywhere.

Grading that reads state

A solve is a change on the simulated machine — a service started, a permission fixed, a file recovered — and the score is recomputed on the server from that state, whatever the browser claims.

Records a student can keep

Passing an attempt issues a completion record with a printed certificate and a verifiable export packet, so the training outlives the session that produced it.

One stack, consumed not rebuilt

OnTrak is a business platform in the Innotel Platform Stack — it consumes the shared services.

Identity & access

Sign-in is Authentik through Cerulean, with a local credential fallback so each app still deploys on its own. Roles and permissions are re-checked on the server for every privileged action.

Secrets & trust

Runtime secrets resolve from Cerulean Vault. Public hosts and the wildcard certificate come from Cerulean; nothing in the repo holds a key, a certificate or a real address.

Storage & evidence

Attachment and evidence bytes sit behind a storage port — local disk for a single node, an object-locked bucket for a desk that has to prove a record was never edited.

Boundaries — what OnTrak owns, consumes and deliberately does not own — are written down in docs/stack.md.
